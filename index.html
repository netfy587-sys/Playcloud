Ready to Discover More? Meet Dominoes

Welcome to Playcloud, an information page about simple pastimes people enjoy at home. Today’s topic is dominoes, a tile-matching activity that has been played across the world for hundreds of years.

This guide covers the tiles, the setup, the matching rules, a few strategy ideas and some care tips, with a picture for each step.

The tiles

Take a Look Today

Dominoes are small rectangular tiles, usually made of wood, plastic or stone. Each tile is divided into two halves by a line, and each half shows a number of dots called pips, from zero (blank) up to six.

The most common set is called double-six. It holds 28 tiles, one for every possible pairing of two numbers from 0 to 6. Tiles with the same number on both halves, such as 4 and 4, are called doubles.

Setup

Explore New Possibilities

Playcloud keeps the setup short, so you can start within a minute.

  1. Shuffle: turn all tiles face down on the table and mix them well.
  2. Draw: with two players, take seven tiles each. With three or four players, take five each. Stand your tiles up so only you can see them.
  3. Boneyard: the tiles left on the table are the “boneyard” or stock, used for drawing later.
  4. Who starts: the player holding the highest double places it first. If nobody has a double, the highest tile starts.

Rules

Find Your Best Match

The idea is simple: join a tile to the chain so that touching ends show the same number.

  1. Take turns clockwise, adding one tile at either end of the chain.
  2. Match the ends: a 2 must touch a 2, a 5 must touch a 5, and so on.
  3. Doubles are placed crosswise, across the line of play.
  4. No match? In the draw version, take tiles from the boneyard until you can play. In the block version, you pass.
  5. End of a round: a round ends when someone plays their last tile, or when nobody can play (a “block”).
  6. Score: add up the pips left in the other players’ hands. Play to an agreed total, such as 100 points.
Strategy

Explore. Choose. Enjoy.

Dominoes mixes luck with careful choices. These ideas help you pick the right tile each turn.

Play heavy tiles early

Tiles with many pips cost more points if left in your hand, so use them first.

Keep variety

Hold tiles with different numbers, so you can match whatever the chain shows.

Watch the ends

Notice which numbers appear often on the table. Those numbers are running out.

Use doubles wisely

A double can only match one number, so play it when that number is open.

Versions

Your Choice, Your Way

Block

No drawing. If you cannot play, you pass. Rounds are fast and tactical.

Draw

Take tiles from the boneyard until you find a match. Good for beginners, since you are never stuck.

Partners

Four players in two teams, with partners sitting opposite. Team scores are added together.

Care tips

Keep tiles dry, wipe them with a soft cloth and store them in their box so none go missing. Play on a flat, smooth table.

Common questions

Can I use a different set size?

Yes. Larger sets such as double-nine have more tiles and suit bigger groups, but the matching rules stay the same.

What if nobody can play?

The round is blocked. Players count the pips in their hands, and the lowest total usually wins that round.

Is it hard to learn?

No. If you can match numbers, you can play. Strategy comes with practice.
